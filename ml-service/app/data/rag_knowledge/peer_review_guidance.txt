Tags: peer review, feedback, quality, critique
Keywords: review, feedback, critique, comment, quality, improve
Content: Useful review feedback is specific and actionable. Saying that something is good or bad tells the author nothing they can work with; naming what specifically works and suggesting one concrete alternative does.
Example: Pick one part of the code and describe what it does well, then one part where a different approach might read more clearly.
Question: Could the author act on your feedback without having to ask what you meant?

Tags: peer review, feedback, reasoning, understanding
Keywords: why, reasoning, understand, explain, intent, review
Content: The most valuable review question asks why a decision was made rather than pointing out what is wrong. It often surfaces a misunderstanding the author did not know they had, and does so without putting them on the defensive.
Example: Choose one section you found hard to follow and ask the author to walk you through their reasoning for it.
Question: What was the original reasoning behind this part of the code?

Tags: peer review, feedback, readability, quality
Keywords: readable, clear, naming, confusing, understand, maintain
Content: Code is read far more often than it is written. A name that made sense while writing a section may say very little to someone meeting it for the first time, and that gap is exactly what a reviewer can see and the author cannot.
Example: Read one section aloud without explaining it, and note where you had to pause to work out what something meant.
Question: Which part of this code took you longest to understand, and why?
